Revisions to the Twilight Imperium: Fourth Edition Living Rules Reference (2.0)
[Note: What follows are unofficial changes to the Living Rules Reference (2.0) for the Blue Reverie homebrew project, assembled while an official update to the Twilight Imperium: Fourth Edition Living Rules Reference remains pending, these revisions are not official rules.]


* Using this reference
* This document is the definitive source for all Twilight Imperium Fourth Edition rules, including all official expansions. The Living Rules Reference may occasionally be updated with additions, clarifications, errata, and an FAQ, changes to which are listed on the preceding pages. Additionally, the related topics of each entry can be clicked to navigate to the associated section.
* The glossary provides players with detailed rules and clarifications listed in alphabetical order by topic. Players are encouraged to use the glossary’s digital bookmarks as well as the index on page [##] to find specific topics within the glossary.
* Twilight Imperium is a social game, and these rules are only the official rules. Players may always (unanimously, of course) agree to adapt, replace, interpret, or simply ignore rules in this document as they find suitable for the game they desire to play.

   1. Abilities
   * .2
If an ability contradicts information in the Rules Reference, that ability takes precedence. If both the ability and the rules can be followed at the same time, they should be.
   * .6(b)
Information in this rules reference does not take precedence over a contradictory ability, even if this reference uses the word “cannot” to describe a rule’s game effect(s).
   * .8(a)
An ability which would "allow" a player to resolve an effect is optional to that player.
   * .18(a)
Abilities that can be resolved “at any time,” “during combat,” “during the action phase,” or similar continuing timing windows can be resolved more than once during that continuing timing window, but not for the same effect during a discrete timing window within that continuing timing window.
   * .18(b)
Non-agent abilities that can be resolved at the start, end, or otherwise during a combat cannot be resolved by players not participating in that combat unless otherwise specified.
   * .19 [consolidation and clarification]
If there are multiple abilities that players wish to resolve at the same time during the same timing window, each player takes a turn resolving an ability in an order determined by the phase. This process continues until each player has resolved each ability that they wish to resolve during that window.
   * .19(a)
During the action phase, players take turns in initiative order, beginning with the active player. 
   * .19(b)
During the strategy and agenda phases, players take turns starting with the speaker and proceeding clockwise. 
   * .19(c)
During the status phase, players take turns in initiative order, if able, and otherwise start with the speaker before proceeding clockwise.
   * .20
An ability that is not resolved cannot be resolved within a timing window generated by that ability.
   * .20(a)
A player cannot satisfy an ability’s timing conditions during the same window they would also resolve that ability. (e.g., a unit placed in a system “when” it is activated cannot be used to satisfy a condition which requires that system to contain that unit “when” it is activated.)
   * .26(a)
If an ability would allow a unit that has a unit ability to "gain" that same unit ability, that unit’s owner may choose to use either the original or gained unit ability, but not both.
   * .28
A player cannot use an ability to destroy their own units unless that effect is part of that ability’s cost, or otherwise specified.
   * .29
Abilities that occur during a continuing timing window (e.g., a turn, phase, or combat) can also be resolved at the “start” and “end” of that timing window.

                              10. Anti-fighter barrage (unit ability)
                              * A unit with the “Anti-Fighter Barrage” ability may be able to destroy an opponent’s fighters at the onset of a space battle. To use the “Anti-Fighter Barrage” ability, such as during the “Anti-Fighter Barrage” step of the first round of space combat, players resolve the following steps:
                              * .1
STEP 1—ROLL DICE: Each player may use the “Anti-Fighter Barrage” ability of each of their units in the active system by rolling a specific number of dice for each of those units; this is called an anti-fighter barrage roll. A hit is produced for each die roll that is equal to or greater than the unit’s anti-fighter barrage value.
                              * .1(b)
The “Anti-Fighter Barrage” ability is displayed as “Anti-Fighter Barrage X (xY).” The X is the minimum value needed for a die to produce a hit, and Y is the number of dice rolled. An “Anti-Fighter Barrage” ability may not be accompanied by a (Y) value; the anti-fighter barrage roll for such a unit consists of one die.
                              * .1(d)
[removed]
                              * .2
STEP 2—ASSIGN HITS: Each player must choose and destroy one of their fighters in the active system for each hit their opponent’s anti-fighter barrage roll produced.

                                                16. Bombardment (unit ability)
                                                * A unit with the “Bombardment” ability may be able to destroy another player’s ground forces during an invasion. To use the “Bombardment” ability, such as during the “Bombardment” step, players resolve the following steps:
                                                * .1
STEP 1—CHOOSE TARGETS AND ROLL DICE: The bombarding player may use the “Bombardment” ability of each of their units in the space area of the active system by choosing which planet each of those units will bombard, and which player's units on that planet to bombard, if any. Then, that player rolls dice for each of those units; this is called a bombardment roll. A hit is produced for each die roll that is equal to or greater than the unit’s “Bombardment” value.
                                                * .1(b)
The “Bombardment” ability is displayed as “Bombardment X (xY).” The X is the minimum value needed for a die to produce a hit, and Y is the number of dice rolled. A “Bombardment” ability ability may not be accompanied by a (Y) value; the bombardment roll for such a unit consists of one die.
                                                * .1(d)
Multiple planets in a system, and player’s units on those planets (if coexisting), may be bombarded, but a player must declare which planet and which player’s units on that planet a unit is bombarding before making a bombardment roll.
                                                * .2
STEP 2—ASSIGN HITS: The player whose units are being bombarded chooses and destroys one of their ground forces on that planet for each hit the bombardment roll produced.

                                                         19. Coexistence
                                                         * Some abilities or game effects instruct a player's units to coexist with another player's units on the same planet. A planet must contain a player's units for other players to coexist on that planet.
                                                         * .1
When a player's units begin coexisting (or otherwise enter into coexistence) on a planet, they remain on that planet. Coexisting units are blockaded and do not participate in combat.
                                                         * .1(a)
A player's units cannot begin coexisting, or enter coexistence without another player to coexist with. That player places a control token on that planet to mark that they control it. 
                                                         * .1(b)
A player who controls coexisting units that are on a planet another player controls is coexisting with that other player.
                                                         * .1(c)
If a player places or moves units onto a planet that contains their coexisting units, those units enter coexistence. If those units are removed from that planet, they stop coexisting.
                                                         * .2
A player who controls coexisting units on a planet is treated as though they control that planet only for the purposes of scoring objectives.
                                                         * .2(a)
Players only consider their own units on planets toward scoring objectives and other game effects.
                                                         * .2(b)
Other players do not treat coexisting players as controlling planets those players are coexisting on for the purposes of scoring objectives.
                                                         * .3
Players who gain control of a planet that contains 1 or more other players' coexisting units gain that planet's associated card(s) as usual.
                                                         * .3(a)
Coexisting units cannot establish control of planets.
                                                         * .3(b)
At any time, if a planet contains only coexisting units, one coexisting player's units stop coexisting according to the applicable timing rules for resolving abilities.
                                                         * .4
During Invasion, the active player may end coexistence for units they control in the active system to commit those units to the planet that contains them.
                                                         * .4(a)
When a player commits any number of units to a planet they control, they may end coexistence for 1 other player’s units on that planet. Those units participate in ground combat, if able.
                                                         * .4(b)
During Invasion, before the active player gains control of any planets, that player may end coexistence for up to 1 other player’s units on each of those planets which were in coexistence at the start of the tactical action, and return to the start of the “Ground Combat” step.

                                                                                                                                       48. Ground combat
                                                                                                                                       * During the “Ground Combat” step of an invasion, if the active player has ground forces on a planet that contains another player’s non-coexisting ground forces, those players resolve a ground combat on that planet. To resolve a ground combat, players perform the following steps:
                                                                                                                                       * .1 
STEP 1—ROLL DICE: Each participating player rolls one die for each ground force they have on the planet; this is called a ground combat roll, or combat roll. If a unit’s combat roll produces a result that is equal to or greater than that unit’s combat value, that roll produces a hit.
                                                                                                                                       * .4
Ground combat ends when only one participating player (or neither participating player) has ground forces on the planet.
                                                                                                                                       * .4(c)
After a combat ends, the participating player with one or more ground forces remaining on the planet is the winner of the combat; the other participating player is the loser of the combat.
                                                                                                                                       * .4(d)
If no participating player has a ground force remaining, there is no winner; the combat ends in a draw.
