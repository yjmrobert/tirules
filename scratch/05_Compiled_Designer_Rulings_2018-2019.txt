Dane: Bribery is worded poorly and should more closely match the style distinguished councilor. The extra wording was added in because whereas the timing of distinguished councilor incorporates that it targets the outcome you voted for, the timing of bribery cannot. It should specify that you are casting 1 additional vote for the outcome that you voted for. The phrasing of 'any' is misleading and can be read as triggering the Golden Rule in regards to what additional votes can be cast toward.         @QuantumOctopus -5 UTC, 05/16/2019
Q: Alice Hacan and Bob Jol-Nar are making a deal. Carol Nekro is waiting on them. Alice has no tokens in her tactical pool, and has Mining Initiative. She has the Technology card unexhausted. Bob has the Warfare strategy card and a Morale Boost. Carol has a Sabotage. On Alice's turn, Bob gives Alice his Morale Boost so that Alice does not play Technology on her turn. This deal is binding, as Bob can immediately give Alice the Morale Boost, and Alice can play Mining Initiative. Alice plays Mining Initiative, and makes a binding deal with Carol who has Sabotage. Alice gives Carol the Morale Boost so that Carol uses Sabotage with Mining Initiative in the process of resolving. This deal is binding as Alice can give Carol Morale Boost, and Carol can use Sabotage. Carol uses Sabotage on Mining Initiative fulfilling the Alice-Carol deal. Mining Initiative does not resolve. Alice can't uphold her end of the deal, as she must play Technology before passing, and can't return the Morale Boost to Bob, as she has already traded it away to Carol. Therefore the game must be forever stalled on Alice's turn.
Possible rulings to resolve the paradox: A) The deal is not binding to begin with. B) The deal is no longer binding after the sabotage. C) The deal was fulfilled when Mining Initiative was first played. D) The sabotage is an illegal play because of the deal. E) The situation is not a paradox; inaction is an option and the game should just be forever stalled. Previous rulings by Dane disprove A. Player autonomy implies that D is also not a solution. The game manual implies that time must progress for a deal to resolve, invalidating E. Therefore, we determine that either B or C is valid. B implies that Sabotage use breaks ALL binding deals. C has the least game changing ramifications. So the real question then is: Does a binding deal last until the the components are superficially or holistically fulfilled? Superficial being just until the action card is played Holistic being until Technology is definitely not played. The only one of the solutions that has any actual backing besides common sense though, is A.
Dane: After some thought, I think that I’d have to go with A—The deal was never binding to begin with, assuming that Alice’s portion of the deal was to “not play the technology strategic action on my turn.” If the deal was just to attempt to play Mining Initiative, on the other hand, then her portion of the deal was fulfilled by the attempt (and thus the answer would be C).


Essentially, this question (and several others in the same vein) have given me sufficient reason to believe that the result of an action card (including, such as in this case, simply successfully resolving that action card in any way) cannot be part of a binding deal. Alice cannot guarantee that she can perform an action other than the technology strategic action as she has no performable actions that don’t require playing an AC to grant. Despite having a component action on Mining Initiative, she has to successfully play that card in order to resolve it, and as a result it isn’t binding. Moreover, timing-wise the terms of the deal are not immediately resolvable at the moment of the transaction. Alice has to successfully resolve the card in order to ‘have’ the component action, creating a timing issue with the deal as well, since both parties are not currently in ‘possession’ of the respective elements of their deal (Bob is, Alice is not).


This may go in the face of a scenario that I have ruled on before, but I think that this is the fairest way to resolve this situation and similar situations. Hopefully this all makes sense.

        3. The combat is considered to be a draw (triggered by “after combat ends,” 38.4)
        4. Daxcive Animators does not trigger (the combat must be won).
        5. The invasion progresses to step 5 (establish control, 44.5)
        6. Neither player has units remaining on the planet, the defender retains control of the planet (44.5)

In the Tabletop Simulator community, the standing rule is that the Sol and Letnev promissaries are OK to redeem even without the requisite tokens, by virtue of the way the cards are worded. However, the L1z1x, Muaat and Xxcha cannot be invoked unless there is a token in the pool. This makes these latter notes less usable and, therefore, less tradable. Was this intentional, or should these five cards be played more consistently with regard to "implied cost"?


Answer: Thanks for your question. A few things to address here. First, the Sol promissory note (the likely source of confusion or at least contradiction)—the line ‘if able’ is extraneous. It isn’t inaccurate, but it doesn’t match the templating of the other similar promissory notes, and that is not by design. Second, 1.15. As you interpreted, this line is about timing rather than obligation. Each of the effects of these should be resolvable whether or not the affected faction has a command token available. I believe this may be in contradiction to a ruling made in the past, but upon further review I believe that this is the most fair/accurate interpretation and also provides equitable worth to the promissory notes of the L1Z1X, Embers, and Xxcha.






        "Hi Dane, I just wanted to verify that in light of the recent rulings re: promissory notes not requiring command tokens, the L1Z1X and Sol players could exchange promissory notes and play both on the Sol's turn to generate infinite turns (via Orbital Drop) and infinite ground forces?"
        Answer: "In light of this finding and others, the previous ruling for these cards will have to stand. I’d have liked the cards to be more playable, but not at the cost of creating infinite loopholes. Apologies for the confusion. The command tokens must be present in order to resolve these PNs, as was the understanding before my recent ruling."
